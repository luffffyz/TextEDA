# Review of the design method, round 1

Artifact: the way a circuit is captured, checked and handed over — the schematic model of the
language, the rules `texteda check` actually applies, and the reference project as the instance
they are exercised on. One role: hardware engineer (a person who signs off on schematics, reads
datasheets, and is paid to find what the tooling does not see). Blind to layout and to the other
roles' notes, per [role-reviews.md](../guides/role-reviews.md). The method is
[role-reviews.md](../guides/role-reviews.md); the record format is
[ADR-0037](../adr/09-view/0037-review-records-and-attribution.md). The round is open: the
accountable person has not written a decision yet.

The reference project was reviewed as it stands in the tree: `design/sch/main.teda` with the three
modules it composes, the component files, and the two `design/pcb/*.teda` descriptions, read
through `texteda check` on 2026-11-16 (0 errors, 196 warnings, 8 notes).

- hardware (2026-11-16): the barrel jack's sleeve is on the wrong net, and nothing in the toolchain can ask about it. `design/sch/modules/charger-power.teda:69` puts `J1.2` (the sleeve, i.e. the adapter's return) on AC_COMMON together with the sources of both input FETs, and `:70` continues that same net with `R17.1` — the resistor that feeds the charger's VCC at `:99`. The charger's own ground (`U1.GND`, `U1.PAD`) is on GND at `:77-79`, and no member of AC_COMMON is on GND, so the adapter's return and the board's ground are two different nets with nothing between them. Per SLUS892D (pin 24), VCC is fed through 10 Ω from the common-source (diode-OR) point of the two input FETs, and that point sits at the adapter's *positive* rail — and as far as the pin functions go, the FET stage in this netlist matches that description, which is what makes the sleeve's net the odd one out. The product even exposes the two separately (`AC_RET AC_COMMON` at `:106`, next to the `GND` port). Either the sleeve belongs on GND, or the design means a separate adapter return — and the language has no way to write that intent: there is no tie device to say "these two nets are one node" ([F12](../backlog.md)), and no rule compares two nets that ought to be one. `texteda check` reports 0 errors on this design. Evidence: `design/sch/modules/charger-power.teda:69-70`, `:77-79`, `:99`, `:106`; `assets/components/connectors/dc-005-20a.teda:16-17`; `design/sch/main.teda:34-38`. Settled by: the datasheet's Figure 20 read against this netlist, and then either a corrected net membership or a tie the language can express.
  → addressed 2026-11-16: the sleeve is on the board's ground now. `J1.2` moved from AC_COMMON to GND (`design/sch/modules/charger-power.teda:81`), and the adapter's return is no longer a separate terminal: `AC_RET` is gone from the module's ports (`:108`), from the root's `nets` and `ports` (`design/sch/main.teda:19`, `:32`), from the module drawing (`views/modules/charger-power.module-view.teda`), and from the spec's example (`spec/module.md` §4). The legacy detail page the renumber and view tests restore drew the port as well and no longer does (`packages/cli/test/fixtures/legacy-charger.view.teda`), which is why the view-metrics expectation now counts three ports. The FET stage and VCC's feed are untouched — AC_COMMON (`:73`) stays the common-source node, now an internal net of the module rather than a product terminal. The second half of the note stands: the language still has no way to say that two nets are one node, so nothing could have caught this, and `check` reports the same 197 diagnostics before and after.

- hardware (2026-11-16): the ERC reads one electrical type, so none of the error classes I sign off on can be caught. `packages/core/src/erc/connectivity.ts:97` is the only place a pin's type is consumed (`pin.type === "no_connect"`); the language defines `input`, `output`, `power_in`, `power_out`, `tri_state` and `open_collector` (`spec/teda.md:561`), and no rule reads any of them. So "a power input with no driver", "two outputs on one net" and "a floating input" are all invisible. The two cases in this design come out as the same generic `net.pin-unassigned` warning as the 78 unused MCU GPIOs: `M2.U1.5` (ENA, declared `input`, connected to nothing — `assets/components/regulators/tps5430ddar.teda:22`) and `M1.J1.3` (the jack's switch contact). A reviewer cannot tell the first two from the other 78 by anything but counting. Evidence: `packages/core/src/erc/connectivity.ts:97`; `spec/teda.md:561`; `assets/components/regulators/tps5430ddar.teda:22`; `texteda check --json` on the reference project (80 `net.pin-unassigned`, 78 of them `M3.U1`). Settled by: a rule that separates an unconnected input from an unconnected passive or output, or a decision that this stays a manual reading and `check` says so instead of staying silent.
  → addressed 2026-11-16: the ERC reads the electrical types now (`packages/core/src/erc/connectivity.ts`), and the rules are in `spec/teda.md` §10 rule 4 with their codes in the registry (`docs/error-codes.md`). Three of them, all off the pin type: two hard drivers (`output` / `power_out`) on one net is `net.output-conflict` (error); a pin of type `input` / `power_in` that is not connected is `net.input-unassigned` (warning), a separate code from the ordinary `net.pin-unassigned`; a `power_in` on a net that nothing sources is `net.power-undriven` (note — the source may sit behind a series element or off-board, so the tool points at the net instead of judging it). What that buys on the reference project: the TPS5430's floating ENA is now its own finding, and the 78 unused MCU GPIOs stay ordinary warnings. Two false positives surfaced and were fixed at the source: the crystal's pins were typed `output` / `input` although a crystal is a passive resonator the MCU's OSC_OUT drives, so the MCU and the crystal looked like two drivers on one net (`assets/components/passives/xtal-8mhz-3225.teda`); and the BQ24610's VCC arrives through R17 from another net, which the tool cannot see — hence a note and not a warning. The language still cannot say "this net is driven from off-board" except through a port, so that half of the note stands as a manual reading.
  → addressed 2026-11-16 (the language half): that remaining half is now a tracked item rather than a reading to remember: [F77](../backlog.md) names the construct to decide — a root-level declaration that a net is fed from outside the board, or a direction on the project port — together with the four things such a construct needs (its name, where it may live, what it does to `net.power-undriven`, and how it lands in the IR, which means an `ir_version` bump with a migration note). The rule itself stays a note: the tool sees one net and cannot judge where the driver is.

- hardware (2026-11-16): every part with more than two pins is outside the rating check, which is every part that matters. `packages/core/src/design/ratings.ts:101` skips any device whose pin count is not 2. On this project `check` reports: "62 of 83 instances were not compared with a declared window: 2 not two-terminal, 46 with no declared voltage capability, 14 on a net without a declared voltage window", and "23 of 27 resistive instances were not compared with a power limit". So the TPS5430's own 28 V limit (`assets/components/regulators/tps5430ddar.teda:14`), the TPS7A2033's input limit — not recorded at all, and the file says so (`assets/components/regulators/tps7a2033pdbvr.teda:16`) — and the charger's VCC window (`design/sch/modules/charger-power.teda:113`) are each compared with nothing. [F42](../backlog.md) already names the hole; what this round adds is the cost: the design's four multi-pin parts carry no electrical verification, and the check result is green. Evidence: `packages/core/src/design/ratings.ts:101`; the two notes in `texteda check --json`; the component files cited. Settled by: a multi-pin stress model (F42), or a decision that `check` must not read as a pass on a design where most parts went uncompared.
  → addressed 2026-11-16: the second way the note offers — a decision that `check` must not read as a pass — is what landed; the multi-pin model itself did not. The report now states the coverage instead of only counting it: the summary's last line is "Not fully checked: 21 of 83 instances were compared with a declared window." and "Not fully checked: 4 of 27 resistive instances were compared with a power limit.", and the summary counts the notes too, which it used to leave out (it said "0 errors, 195 warnings" and dropped the three notes behind them). The same numbers travel in the machine payload under `coverage` (`stress` / `dissipation`, each with `compared` and `total`), so the line a person reads and the payload a host's tool registry reads cannot disagree (ADR-0044 D2). The checker now returns its own coverage (`packages/core/src/design/ratings.ts`), so the numbers are computed once; the payload carries it (`packages/cli/src/check.ts`), and the summary prints it (`packages/cli/scripts/texteda.ts`). Two smaller things fell out of the same decision: the comparison is a schematic-level check — it reads the root's nets, the declared windows and the component library, never a PCB description — so it no longer hangs off the PCB branch and runs for every project with a logical root; and the exit code is still zero, because an incomplete comparison is a note and not an error (ADR-0026 D13) — what changed is that the report can no longer be read as a pass. The decision is recorded in [ADR-0026](../adr/07-devices/0026-a-part-states-its-own-ratings.md) D13, the rule in `spec/teda.md` §7.3. What is still open is the model: the terminal pair, the role's direction and `kind`, and the datasheet values ([F42](../backlog.md)) — the TPS5430, the TPS7A2033, the BQ24610 and the CH32V307 still carry no electrical verification. The reference project's numbers are unchanged (21 of 83 and 4 of 27); what changed is that they are in the report's last line instead of inside a note.
  → addressed 2026-11-16 (the residual question): whether an incomplete comparison should change the exit code is now a tracked item rather than an open decision: [F78](../backlog.md) lays out the three shapes — keep exit 0; exit non-zero whenever the comparison is incomplete (every project without a window on every net fails, including the correct ones); or a third state that separates "wrong" from "not fully checked" — and what each would cost. The report does not change again until that is decided.

- hardware (2026-11-16): a real conflict is written down in the repository and reported by nothing. The jack is rated `rated_current current 500mA` (`assets/components/connectors/dc-005-20a.teda:21`) against a design whose adapter limit is 4 A, and the component file says so in a comment (`assets/components/connectors/dc-005-20a.teda:14`: "The 500mA rating is below charger-power's stated 4A adapter limit; this jack is not rated for the design's full input current") — a comment is not a rule. The `current` constraint that would catch it (`current VBAT 3A`, `design/sch/main.teda:51`) sits on the battery net, and no rule consumes a `current` constraint at all. The facts are all here; the conflict is not. Evidence: `assets/components/connectors/dc-005-20a.teda:14`, `:21`; `design/sch/main.teda:51`; `packages/core/src/design/ratings.ts:226` (dissipation only). Settled by: a rule comparing a net's `current` constraint with the `rated_current` of the parts on that net, or a procurement rule refusing a part rated below the current the net declares.
  → addressed 2026-11-16: the `current` constraint is consumed now, and the conflict is reported instead of commented. The rule compares what a part is rated for — a part's own `ratings` (`rated_current`, a datasheet fact) or a generic's line column (a requirement) — with what the design declares. The first half of the note was a design gap and is closed: the 4 A adapter limit moved out of the module's header comment and into a declared fact (`current ADAPTER_P 4A`, `design/sch/modules/charger-power.teda:119`), so the facts are in the IR instead of in prose. What `check` now says: "net ADAPTER_P declares 4A, and M1.J1 (dc-005-20a) on it is rated 0.5A" (`design.current-below-rating`, warning). The severity is a warning and not an error because the tool cannot attribute a net's current to a member — the language does not say which member of a net carries it (ADR-0026, Open Points), and the reviewer's own note says the constraint sat on the wrong net for exactly that reason. Where both sides are pinned down — the limit is on a pin and the part is two-terminal — the comparison is certain and it is an error (`design.current-exceeded`): the design says what that pin carries, and a two-terminal part's rating is about the path between its two pins. A part with more pins has a rating about a path the language does not name yet (ADR-0026 D4), so it stays a warning; a part that sits on two rails declaring different currents is not compared with either (it is a converter or a series element, and the language cannot say which — a series element carries the same current on both sides); a part that states no `rated_current` is counted in `design.current-unknown` rather than passed. The rule is in `spec/teda.md` §7.3, the codes in `docs/error-codes.md`, the checker in `packages/core/src/design/current.ts`. What is not closed: the language still cannot say which member of a net carries its current, so the net-subject comparison stays a warning, and the reviewer's other option — a procurement rule refusing a part rated below the current — does not reach this part, because a connector has no generic line to fill (`rated_current` is a column only for the inductor constructions). That half is tracked as [F80](../backlog.md).

- hardware (2026-11-16): the same window is written twice, in the root and in the module, and nothing checks that the two agree. The compiled IR carries `voltage VOUT 4.5V..5.5V`, `voltage GND 0V..0V`, `voltage V3V3 3.0V..3.6V` and `current V3V3 300mA` **twice** each — once from `design/sch/main.teda:46-53` and once from the mcu-board module (`design/sch/modules/mcu-board.teda:62-65`), because the module's `VIN_5V` merges into the root's `VOUT` net. One fact, two homes: changing the 5 V rail means editing two files, and if one is missed, nothing says so. Evidence: `design/sch/main.teda:49`, `:50`, `:53`; `design/sch/modules/mcu-board.teda:62`, `:64`, `:65`; the constraints in `texteda compile examples/reference-project main --out …`. Settled by: a rule that a module-local constraint and the project-level constraint on the same net must agree, or a decision that only the root declares windows.
  → addressed 2026-11-16: the rule is there, and the second home is now visible. One net states one window: two declarations of the same net that disagree are `net.constraint-conflict` (error — the design says two different things about one net), and two that agree are `net.constraint-duplicate` (note — one fact, two homes, which is what the note is about), with both places named so the reader knows which two files to reconcile (`design/sch/main.teda:47` and `design/sch/modules/mcu-board.teda:62`, and so on). The reference project reports the four duplicates it has — `voltage GND`, `voltage VOUT`, `voltage V3V3` and `current V3V3` — and 0 conflicts, so the design agrees with itself today; what the note complained about is that nothing would say so if it stopped agreeing. One consequence worth stating: when two windows of one net conflict, the voltage comparison no longer picks whichever came first — that net's window is unknown and the parts on it are counted as not compared, because a design that contradicts itself has no window to compare against (ADR-0026 D13). The rule is in `spec/teda.md` §7.3, the codes in `docs/error-codes.md`, the checker in `packages/core/src/design/constraints.ts`. The other half of the settled-by — a decision that only the root declares windows — is not taken: the module's window is a statement about the module's own rail, and which home wins is a design decision the reference project has not made; the note now says so instead of leaving it silent. It is tracked as [F79](../backlog.md), with the shape of the four duplicates written out there.

- hardware (2026-11-16): the board does not implement the module the root assigns to it, and the gate that should catch it reports one summary line. `design/sch/main.teda:9` assigns M1 (charger-power) and M2 (buck-5v) to the charger-buck board, and `design/pcb/charger-buck.teda:6-8` lists both modules — but `place` (`:21-30`) holds only M2's nine parts and not one of M1's fifty-three. `check` says: "PCB charger-buck places 9 of its 62 physical instances; 53 not placed: M1.C1, M1.C2, … (45 more)". One warning, seven names, "(45 more)"; the board that would come off the line is a buck converter with no charger in it, and it reads like a finished board. Evidence: `design/sch/main.teda:9`; `design/pcb/charger-buck.teda:6-8`, `:21-30`; `texteda check --json` (`bind.instance-unplaced`). Settled by: the binding gate failing rather than warning when a board declares a module it does not place, or the report naming every missing instance.

- hardware (2026-11-16): the copper is never checked against the current the design declares. The root declares `current VOUT 3A` (`design/sch/main.teda:52`), and the board declares `rules width 0.3` (`design/pcb/charger-buck.teda:83`) and carries that 3 A rail on 0.3 mm traces (`design/pcb/charger-buck.teda:65-67`). The two facts live in different files, and nothing in the language relates a net's current to the width of the copper on it — there is no rule that reads them together, and `rules` has no current-carrying form. Evidence: `design/sch/main.teda:52`; `design/pcb/charger-buck.teda:65-67`, `:83`; `spec/pcb.md` §4 (the `rules` segment). Settled by: a rule comparing a net's declared current with the width of the traces carrying it, or a decision that trace sizing stays a layout job and `check` only reports on it.
